January is still the cruise industry's most important booking month. Promotions cluster, deposits drop, and the calendar for the next 12–18 months comes into focus. December is when you decide *what* you will book so Wave Season is a transaction, not a research project.

You do not need to book everything in January. You should know which categories historically get more expensive—or more scarce—if you wait.

## What to Put on the January List

**Summer Europe (especially Western Med and Greek Isles)**
Balcony cabins and popular dates for next summer are not leftover inventory in March. Wave Season is when many of those bookings should be deposited, not discovered.

**Alaska 2027**
Peak-week Glacier Bay and Hubbard sailings, plus connecting cabins for families, tighten early. January promotions often include onboard credit that makes a good cabin a better total value.

**Holiday 2027 (Christmas and New Year's)**
It feels early. It is not. These sailings follow a scarcity curve, not a last-minute discount curve.

**Repositioning and longer voyages**
Transatlantic, Panama Canal, and World Cruise segments sometimes see their best combination of fare and perks when lines are filling the year ahead. If a crossing is on your list, January is a serious look.

**The sailing you already know you want**
If you have a ship, a month, and a cabin type in mind, Wave Season is for locking it and stacking promotions—not for starting from zero.

## What Can Often Wait

- Off-peak Caribbean weeks in late summer or early fall, if you are flexible
- Newer ships that still have abundant inventory
- Sailings you are only considering because an ad appeared

Waiting is a strategy only when the sailing is not scarce. Treating every cruise like it will be cheaper in April is how people lose the category they wanted.

## How to Use Wave Season Without Getting Played

- Compare **total cost**, including the packages being waived or bundled
- Confirm whether the fare is refundable enough for your risk tolerance
- Ask about price protection or future cruise credits if the line drops the rate
- Do not stretch to a ship you would not book at the pre-sale fare

January noise is loud on purpose. A shortlist of two or three sailings beats a weekend of tab-hopping.

## A Simple December Homework Assignment

Before New Year's:

1. Pick the region and month.
2. Pick two ships that fit the people traveling.
3. Note the cabin type you will actually occupy.
4. Check passport validity.
5. Talk to an advisor or set a booking date in the first half of January.

That is enough. You do not need a spreadsheet of the entire industry.

## Expert Insight

The travelers who benefit most from Wave Season are the ones who already did the fit work—ship, dates, budget—in December. January then becomes execution: promotions, deposits, and monitoring. The travelers who start researching on January 2 often book a headline fare and a mismatch.

## Final Thoughts

Prices do not rise on every sailing, every year, in a straight line. Inventory on the *best* versions of Europe, Alaska, and holidays does get thinner. If those trips are your 2027 plans, January is still the month to stop browsing. Use the next two weeks to decide. Use Wave Season to book.
