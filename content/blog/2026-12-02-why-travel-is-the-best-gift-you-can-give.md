Most holiday gifts have a short half-life. Travel is one of the few presents that expands after you open it: a date on the calendar, a shared story, and a reason to look forward to the year instead of recovering from December.

That is the case for giving a trip. The execution is where well-meaning gifts go wrong.

## Why Experiences Win

Research on happiness and memory consistently favors experiences over objects—especially experiences you share. A cruise or a planned getaway also solves a practical holiday problem: you are not adding clutter to someone else's house.

For couples, parents, and adult children, a trip can be the gift that says *I want time with you*, which a sweater rarely does.

## How to Give Travel Without Creating a Burden

**Do not gift a nonrefundable surprise sailing.**
Surprises collide with work calendars, school, passports, and motion sickness. Gift the *intention* and the budget, then plan together—or confirm dates before you deposit.

**Put real money behind it.**
A "we should go to the Caribbean sometime" card is a wish. A held cabin, a travel credit, or a paid planning session with an advisor is a gift.

**Match the trip to the recipient, not to your bucket list.**
Alaska in a tiny cabin is not a gift to someone who wants a beach. An 18+ ship is not a gift to grandparents traveling with grandkids.

**Include the unglamorous parts.**
Airfare, the hotel night before embarkation, and insurance are part of the present. Gifting only the cruise fare can leave the recipient with a bill.

## Cruise-Specific Ways to Give

- **A cabin deposit for a sailing you have already aligned on dates**
- **An onboard credit** for a trip they already booked (spa, specialty dining, a better internet package)
- **A prepaid specialty dinner or shore excursion** as a "day of" gift
- **A future cruise certificate** or advisor-held credit toward a 2027 sailing during Wave Season
- **For families:** connecting cabins so the gift is *space*, not just a destination

A printed itinerary in a card works if the trip is booked. A vague brochure does not.

## Timing

December 2 is late for a Christmas-week surprise cruise and early enough to make Wave Season (January) the actual gift: "this January we book *your* trip." That framing is often more useful than forcing a sailing over the holidays when everyone is already exhausted.

Passport validity, school calendars, and peak-season availability still apply. A gifted trip with no dates can sit unused. Set a planning deadline.

## Expert Insight

The best travel gifts are collaborative. The giver funds and removes friction. The recipient still gets a say in ship, dates, and pace. That combination feels generous instead of controlling—and it is how cruises actually get taken rather than discussed.

## Final Thoughts

If you want a holiday gift that does not need storage space, give time in another place. Make it specific enough to be real and flexible enough to be kind. A cruise is simply one of the easiest versions of that idea: one booking, a clear date, and a week that becomes the story people tell next December.
